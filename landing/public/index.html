The fall of the last hive

Reyklund was worth a war because of what lay in its rock. Diamantine, refined in the great hive-refineries, bought the Imperium its loyalty and its enemies their interest. For years the planet bled for it.

Then the combatants left. Regiments embarked, warhosts withdrew, the Charter Masters loaded what they could carry and were gone. The world was abandoned to its fate, and the last hive still being fought over was struck from the void by a Space Hulk.

The hive did not die. It broke. Hab-blocks lean over the chasms at angles no architect intended. Refineries smoulder. Somewhere above the smog, a few warp-capable ships still lie at anchor, and passage costs more than any survivor owns.

So the warbands go down into the ruins with lasguns and cinder blocks, to pry loose enough refined diamantine to buy a way off-world. Most of them, at least.

Why they stay

Escape

Most warbands are here to fund a way out. Every Throne they scrape together is a step closer to a berth on one of the last ships.

Plunder

Some came to sack what the armies left behind: refined diamantine in the rubble, Archaeotech in sealed vaults, the hoards of noble houses that fled.

Slaughter

And some have no interest in leaving at all. In a hive with no law and no witnesses, there are things that come only for the killing.

What lies in the ruins

After every fight your heroes search the wreckage. Most of the time it's rubble and a few Thrones. Sometimes it's this:

  • A sump hole with a shard at the bottom, and tainted water
  • A shattered refinery, still guarded
  • A Mag-Lev line that somehow still hums
  • A cargo-lift scrawled with sigils nobody should read
  • The underhive catacombs, and what waits in them
